1 · The live design portfolio is consent-stage, not construction-stage

Five of the six live capital commissions sit at TII Phase 3 or in statutory process: N/M20 Cork to Limerick, the Donegal TEN-T Priority Route, the N3 Virginia Bypass, the N72/N73 Mallow Relief Road and the R132 Drogheda active travel scheme. Luas Finglas has cleared consent, with its Railway Order operative from 13 April 2026. The immediate technical risk sits in environmental and business-case defensibility rather than in construction delivery, and the immediate commercial risk is that fee drawdown stalls at approval gates outside Egis's control.

2 · Operations is the revenue floor and the technical governance blind spot

Three live operations contracts run to the early 2030s: the Dublin Tunnel, Jack Lynch Tunnel and Motorway Operations Control Centre contract, worth up to €600 million excluding VAT over a full sixteen-year term; MMaRC Network C covering roughly 328km of the M7, M8, M9, N10, N20, N22, N24, N25, N28 and N40; and the East-Link Toll Bridge in Dublin Docklands. These sit in different legal entities from the consultancy, and the interface between operational assurance and consultancy-side technical governance is undefined in the public record. Defining it is a task for you.

3 · The credentials base is deep, but it is filed under other names

Forty-seven identifiable schemes span national roads and motorways, secondary roads, bridges, active travel, light and heavy rail, tunnels and transport planning. The great majority were delivered as JB Barry & Partners or Barry Transportation, and the per-scheme evidence lives on two unmaintained legacy websites. Those pages are the spine of every future bid and should be archived locally before the domains lapse.

4 · Egis holds all four classic consultancy roles, which most competitors cannot claim

The record evidences lead designer under the TII Project Management Guidelines, contractor's designer on design-and-build PPPs, employer's representative with PSDP and site supervision, and technical advisor through planning. Adding motorway and tunnel operations to that gives a design-plus-operate proposition that pure consultancies cannot match. Bids score on demonstrated role, not scheme name, so this should be structured as a role matrix rather than a project list.

5 · The senior transport structure was still being built when you were hired

Andrew Doyle, the former Irish transport lead, left for WSP in early 2026 and no like-for-like public successor has been named. The January 2026 reorganisation put Francois Basselot in as Managing Director for UK and Ireland with Mike Birch as Transportation Director. Eamon Daly and Liam Prendiville are the senior Dublin-resident transport figures on the record. Egis was simultaneously advertising for a Director of Urban Transport in Dublin. Your remit is likely to be partly defined by you rather than handed to you.

13 · Regulatory and assurance landscape

What the technical authority is accountable for

The consent sequence, and where the gates sit

The TII Project Management Guidelines phases, with the Infrastructure Guidelines gates that sit alongside them. The two vocabularies are not the same thing and do not map one to one, which is the root of the Mallow terminology conflict in section 12. Diamonds are approval gates; the shaded phases are where five of the six live design commissions currently sit.

Three consent routes, three different exposures. Which route a scheme takes determines who decides it, how long it takes, and what a challenge looks like. All three are live in the current portfolio.

Process and consent

  • TII Project Management Guidelines, Phases 1 to 4, and the phase-gate approvals that govern them
  • Infrastructure Guidelines gates, notably Gate 1 and the preliminary business case, which sit alongside but are not the same as TII phases
  • TII DN design and DM standards publication series, and the scheme departures register
  • Consent routes via An Coimisiún Pleanála, formerly An Bord Pleanála, including Railway Orders for light rail and compulsory purchase orders
  • Part 8 local authority consent for active travel and urban realm schemes
  • Environmental Impact Assessment and Appropriate Assessment obligations
  • DMURS and the National Cycle Manual for urban and active travel design

Risk and duty

  • Judicial review exposure on road schemes. The N6 Galway quashing and the live N2 Slane proceedings are the cautionary cases
  • The Road Safety Audit regime, including the NRA and TII framework under which Egis holds a position
  • Safety, Health and Welfare at Work (Construction) Regulations designer duties and Project Supervisor Design Process appointments, which Egis has held directly on the N56 Donegal scheme
  • Climate Action Plan and NIFTI implications. The requirement to assess climate impact is what quashed the first N6 Galway approval
  • Tunnel safety, fire and life safety, and ventilation assurance arising from the ERTO operations contracts
  • Professional indemnity and designer liability across four separate legal entities
The specific assurance question this portfolio raises. Five of the six live design commissions are technical advisor or Phase 1 to 4 appointments, where the deliverable is a consent application rather than a construction design. The assurance regime for that work is different from construction-stage design assurance: the risk is not buildability but evidentiary robustness under cross-examination, at an oral hearing or in judicial review, potentially years after the work was signed off. Confirm in month one that the technical assurance regime distinguishes the two, and that environmental, climate and traffic modelling deliverables carry the same review rigour as structural calculations.
